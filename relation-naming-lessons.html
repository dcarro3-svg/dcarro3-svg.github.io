Overview

Comprehension is a teachable skill.

Most word problem instruction skips straight to computation. Students learn to hunt for keywords and apply procedures — but they never learn to read a problem structurally. Relation Naming Lessons fix that.

Students learn to identify the type of mathematical relation a problem describes — part-part-whole, comparison, equal groups, ratio — and to name it precisely before ever writing an equation. This is the comprehension step that computation depends on.

The lessons are structured, cumulative, and built for whole-class instruction or independent study. Each lesson introduces a new relation type, builds vocabulary, and provides guided practice with immediate feedback.

Lesson Sequence

Unit 01

Simple Relations

Part-part-whole and additive comparison. Students learn to distinguish "joining" from "comparing" and name each relation type using precise language.

Unit 02

Compound Relations

Multiplicative and ratio relations. Students extend their relational vocabulary to problems involving equal groups, rates, and proportional comparison.

Unit 03

Complex Relations

Multi-step problems with embedded relations. Students practice identifying nested relation structures and mapping them before selecting a solution strategy.

Unit 04

Word Problem Application

Applied practice with authentic word problems. Students use relation naming as a reading comprehension strategy — identifying structure before computing.

The Premise

Students don't fail math
because it's too hard.

They fail because no one taught them to read it. A word problem is a text — it describes a situation with a relational structure. Before a student can solve it, they need to comprehend it. Relation naming is that comprehension step.

These lessons were designed around that premise: teach the structure first, and computation becomes a choice, not a guess.
